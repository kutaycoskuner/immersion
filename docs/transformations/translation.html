II. description · translation

Begin: the cube and a point

The cube is centred at the origin, 2 units wide. We follow a point ppp (the dot in the viewport), at first one of its corners; type another one into the matrix panel.

p=[1.001.001.001.00]{\color{#ffb347}p} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.00 \\ 1.00 \\ 1.00 \\ 1.00 \end{bmatrix}p=​1.001.001.001.00​​

The 4th coordinate w=1w = 1w=1 marks it as a point. These homogeneous coordinates are what lets a 4×44 \times 44×4 matrix move it.

Step 1: the translation matrix

The offset sits in the last column; the rest is the identity, so nothing turns or scales.
