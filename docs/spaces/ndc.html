II. description · clip → NDC

Clip space to NDC: the divide by w

The step after camera to clip: every clip-space point is divided by its own www. The result is the normalized device coordinates (NDC): everything the camera can see lands in the cube from −1-1−1 to 111.

Not a matrix: each point gets its own divisor, its depth www. That is what makes far things small -- the perspective -- and what squeezes the frustum (the pyramid in the viewport) into the cube.

The corner picture is what this camera sees: the cube's xxx and yyy are exactly the place in that picture. What lies outside the frustum (grey, the boxes beyond far and behind the eye) never appears in it.

Squeeze, then mirror

In camera space in front is −z-z−z; in NDC a bigger zzz is farther (near −1-1−1, far +1+1+1), so the depth test can ask "smaller zzz wins". So PPP holds two things: SSS squeezes and keeps in front at −z-z−z, FFF mirrors zzz. NDC is left-handed. The mirror is a sign convention, not a change of shape:

Not yet 2D

Depth goes along in every step so far: PPP has an inverse, and the divide keeps zzz. The picture becomes 2D only when the screen reads xxx and yyy as the place in the picture; zzz goes to the depth test (what is in front).

Self experimentation and comprehension

put ppp on the near plane, z=−nearz = -\text{near}z=−near: its zzz in NDC is exactly −1-1−1

move ppp farther away: its xxx and yyy in NDC shrink -- the perspective

widen the fov: the boxes in the cube get smaller, more fits in

move near closer to 000: the depth range crowds even more towards +1+1+1
