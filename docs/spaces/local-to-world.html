II. description · local → world

Coordinate spaces

A space is an origin and three axes. The same point has different numbers in different spaces: what changes is where you measure from and along which directions.

Local space (object space): the object as it was modelled, around its own origin. The cube is always 2 wide here, its corners at (±1,±1,±1)(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)(±1,±1,±1) -- whatever happens to it in the scene.

World space: the space the whole scene shares (the grid and the faint world axes in the viewport). Every object is placed into it by its own model matrix.

The orange box is a second object: modelled the same way around its own origin, with its own local axes, but placed by its own model matrix (fixed here). Both meet in one world -- that is what world space is for.

The ground is the xzxzxz plane (y=0y = 0y=0). The cube is 2 high in local space, so with its position yyy equal to its scale yyy it stands on the ground once it is placed (t=1t = 1t=1).

Local to world is a 3D to 3D transformation, and it can be undone: world to local is its inverse. That is the difference from a projection (3D to 2D).

Self experimentation and comprehension

only change the position: the cube moves, its local axes stay parallel to the world's

turn it 90∘90^\circ90∘ about yyy: its local xxx axis now points along the world's −z-z−z

the point's local numbers never change while the cube moves: only its world numbers do

a scale of 000 flattens the cube and the way back is lost: no inverse, like a projection
